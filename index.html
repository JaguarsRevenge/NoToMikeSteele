2 · Housing

The Chamber, the rentals, and your rent

Housing cost is the top concern for families in the Chelan valley. Short-term vacation rentals take homes off the long-term market, and Chelan County has spent years fighting over how to limit them.

Steele has been Executive Director of the Lake Chelan Chamber of Commerce for more than 20 years, including all ten of his years in the Legislature. The Chamber is one of the groups that lobbied Chelan County on its short-term-rental rules. Chambers in the county are funded largely through lodging tax.

This cycle, Steele also took $2,400 from Airbnb, the maximum allowed.

Holding a job outside the Legislature is legal and normal in Washington, and Steele discloses it. Nobody has accused him of breaking any rule. But when you ask whose interests he weighs on housing, it is fair to know who pays him and who funds his campaign.

Maggie Adams supports affordable housing with limits on short-term rentals.

3 · Taxes

Who he protects when the bill comes

Washington has one of the most upside-down tax systems in the country. Working families pay a larger share of their income in sales and property taxes than the wealthiest households do. On every chance to change that, Steele has sided with the top.

The district has also changed. The 2024 court-ordered map moved Douglas, Grant and Okanogan counties out of the 12th and added Monroe, Sultan, Gold Bar, North Bend and Snoqualmie. In the August primary the Position 1 race was decided by 174 votes out of more than 44,000. This district is competitive now. It deserves a representative who has to earn it.
