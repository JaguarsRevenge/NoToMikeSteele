2 · Housing

The Chamber, the rentals, and your rent

Housing cost is the top concern for families in the Chelan valley. Short-term vacation rentals take homes off the long-term market, and Chelan County has spent years fighting over how to limit them. The county adopted limits in 2021 and rewrote them in early 2025.Chelan County

Steele has been Executive Director of the Lake Chelan Chamber of Commerce for more than 20 years, including all ten of his years in the Legislature.His bio The Residents Coalition of Chelan County, a residents' group that fought to keep the county's 2021 rental limits, names the Lake Chelan Chamber among the groups that lobbied for the rewrite.Residents Coalition The Chamber's contract with the City of Chelan pays it up to $750,000 a year, pegged to the city's lodging-tax collections, the tax on overnight stays.All Things Lake Chelan

This cycle Steele took $2,400 from Airbnb, the most it could give for the primary and general combined.PDC report On September 18 he took $1,200 from the Washington Multi-Family Housing Association, the trade group for apartment owners and managers.PDC report

Holding a job outside the Legislature is legal and normal in Washington, and Steele discloses it. Nobody has accused him of breaking any rule. But when you ask whose interests he weighs on housing, it is fair to know who pays him and who funds his campaign.

Maggie Adams supports affordable housing with limits on short-term rentals.

3 · Taxes

Who he protects when the bill comes

Washington has the second most upside-down tax system in the country. The poorest fifth of households pay 13.8% of their income in state and local taxes. The top 1% pay 4.1%.ITEP On the Legislature's big votes to ask the wealthiest to pay more, Steele voted no.

SB 6346 · 2026

Income tax on the highest earners ("millionaires' tax")

Voted no after a 24-hour floor debate and called it "the wrong direction."Roll callHis statement

VOTED NO
SB 5096 · 2021

Capital gains tax on large investment profits

Voted no. It applies only to gains above $250,000 a year, and ITEP credits it with moving Washington off the very bottom of its fairness ranking. In 2024 Steele called it "essentially an income tax" and pushed for a hearing on the initiative to repeal it. Voters rejected the repeal.Roll callHis statement

VOTED NO
SB 5126 · 2021

Climate Commitment Act, which makes the largest polluters pay for their emissions

Voted no. Voters later rejected a 2024 initiative to repeal it.Roll call2024 result

VOTED NO

Maggie Adams supports a millionaires' tax and tax reform that makes large corporations pay their share. She wants that revenue to fund universal health care and child care, fully funded schools, and better teacher pay.

4 · Your rights

Four chances to protect your freedom to decide. Four no votes.

Before and after the Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade in 2022, Washington passed laws to protect abortion access and the patients and doctors who rely on it. Steele voted against each of these bills on final passage. All four passed without him.

HB 1851 · 2022

Preserving access to abortion care

Updated the state's abortion-rights law so more trained health care providers can offer care, and barred prosecuting people who help a patient get it.Roll call

VOTED NO
HB 1469 · 2023

Protecting reproductive and gender-affirming health care

Shields patients and doctors in Washington from other states' subpoenas and prosecutions.Roll call

VOTED NO
SB 5768 · 2023

Protecting access to abortion medication

Let the state stockpile mifepristone after a federal court threatened to pull it from the market.Roll call

VOTED NO
SB 5599 · 2023

Supporting young people seeking protected health care

Lets youth shelters notify the state, instead of parents, when a young person staying there is seeking reproductive or gender-affirming care.Roll call

VOTED NO

Each vote is the House final-passage roll call, as republished by LegiScan from the Legislature's official record.

Maggie Adams supports reproductive freedom and equal rights for LGBTQ+ Washingtonians.

Eight years without a real choice

The district has also changed. The 2024 court-ordered map moved Douglas, Grant and Okanogan counties out of the 12th and added Monroe, Sultan, Gold Bar, North Bend and Snoqualmie. In the August primary the Position 1 race was decided by 174 votes out of more than 44,000. This district is competitive now. It deserves a representative who has to earn it.
